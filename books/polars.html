Polars: The Definitive Guide

Jeroen Janssens, Thijs Nieuwdorp

Overview

Polars is a competitor to Pandas written in Rust. Being built in Rust, its execution is much faster than Pandas. The idea for Polars came after Ritchie Vink was trying to work with CSVs in Rust, specifically trying to merge files. Polars: The Definitive Guide covers everything you need to get started and to transition from Pandas to Polars.

The book is divided into several parts. The first covers why Polars is faster and how to transition best. The second introduces the data structures, the eager and lazy APIs, and basic I/O. The third is where Polars shines: expressions. Expressions are also why Polars can be easier than Pandas — the syntax is extremely intuitive. The fourth part covers transforming data: selecting and creating columns, filtering and sorting, working with different data types, summarising and aggregating, joining and concatenating, and reshaping. The final part shows how Polars dataframes can be visualised with tools such as hvPlot or GreatTables, and how Polars can be extended by writing new methods in Rust and making them available in Python.

Key Takeaways

I recently made the switch at work from Pandas to Polars and I can never look back. Polars is so much easier to use and so much faster. Because of how approachable it is, I have even become interested in learning Rust to see how I could extend it for specific use cases and experiment with ML algorithms rewritten in Rust.

Who Should Read This

If you are using Pandas and have struggled with its API, this is what you are looking for.

Rating

5 out of 5. A great entry point to understand what Polars can do. If you need more detail, the online documentation is always there to complement it.
