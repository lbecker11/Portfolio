Practical Statistics for Data Scientists

Peter Bruce & Andrew Bruce

Overview

A while ago there was a HumbleBundle deal on books about data science, analytics, and engineering. Of this series, Practical Statistics for Data Scientists was one of my favourites. It goes into just the right amount of detail about distributions and significance testing. The explanation of the distributions was something I could have definitely used during my statistics classes at university. From there the approach to explaining significance testing was just right. The introduction of permutation testing and the explanation of how p-values can be derived from it was so well elaborated. From there the book moves into the usual ML explanations about supervised and unsupervised learning. For unsupervised learning it covers the usual clustering through k-means and PCA. PCA is something I learned about in linear algebra but which clicked even more through finance and the later book in the series, Data Science: The Hard Parts. For supervised learning it covers the usual regression, classification, and ensemble methods. For classification, Fisher's linear discriminant is something I have not seen covered by many of the other books.

Key Takeaways

Sampling distributions, significance testing through permutation tests.

Who Should Read This

If you perform data science or want to get into it, this will give you a good start on many of the important topics.

Rating

5 out of 5, one of my favourites.
