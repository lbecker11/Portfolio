The Recursive Book of Recursion

Al Sweigart

Overview

Recursion is one of those concepts that tends to be hard to grasp for developers. The actual recursive part is not the main issue but understanding how to divide a problem into a repetitive subproblem. In functional programming recursion is an essential idea, and also in metaprogramming when looking at compile time functions. Al Sweigart works through the classic algorithms and problems and solves them through recursion, making use of memoization and dynamic programming. Most of the problems solved here are graph problems. Because each chapter focuses on specific problems, it is a good idea to try and repeat them from memory. This allows you to see where you are struggling.

Key Takeaways

Recursion is hard, but not because of understanding the call stack, but because of figuring out how a problem can be divided in an efficient way.

Who Should Read This

It's not essential since every problem that can be solved with recursion can also be solved without it. For this reason I think you should read this if you are just curious.

Rating

4 out of 5.
