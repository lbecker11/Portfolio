The Intelligent Investor

Benjamin Graham

Overview

The Intelligent Investor is probably a must read for anyone who wants to learn about investing, even if its knowledge might be outdated by now. I emphasize investing, because Graham draws a clear line between speculating and investing. He approaches the topic from the defensive and enterprising perspective. The defensive approach is simply following the index, where the enterprising perspective requires more active work. A key concept of the book is finding cheap stocks through a good P/E ratio. While this niche has mostly dried up, the main idea of valuing companies based on their real value is still common and essential when looking at long term investing. One of the things Graham might have gotten wrong was his take on warrants. Warrants are what made Edward O. Thorp one of the first real quants. Warrants would turn into options and make up the majority of liquidity we have today. This is the one part I would dispute, but other than that I think it's still an amazing read to get a good starting point about building long term positions.

Key Takeaways

Margin of safety. This part has stuck with me the most. Have a wide portfolio of companies that you believe will continue to perform well. Even if most of them don't, as long as the ones that do outperform the rest, you will be profitable. This connects well to any form of probability math in games or investing.

Who Should Read This

Everyone.

Rating

4 out of 5. I disagree with some takes, but most of the book is essential reading that anyone should have done at some point.
