Data Science: The Hard Parts

Daniel Vaughan

Overview

To get the most out of this book you need to have read the essential books first and ideally have some practice in data science. This book goes into detail about things to consider once the basic model is running. In Data Science: The Hard Parts you learn about how to create value through ML models, what growth decomposition is, creating narratives, omitted variable bias, latent variable models, Frisch-Waugh-Lovell, data leakages, incrementality, and A/B tests. Vaughan went into detail about how wrong assumptions can arise from correlation and how features can be broken down into orthogonal components through processes like PCA. This is essential to risk management in finance, for example, where returns are broken down into several principal components. At the end the book looks at how the role of a data scientist might develop due to AI. What becomes more important in the future is that hard skills can be handled by AI but what it cannot take away are the soft skills: business knowledge, presentation skills, and networking. These are skills that need to be honed going forward.

Key Takeaways

Omitted variable bias and latent features. Breaking down features into their orthogonal components.

Who Should Read This

Once you know the basics of data science this is a logical follow up.

Rating

5 out of 5, the explanation of latent features was essential to my understanding.
