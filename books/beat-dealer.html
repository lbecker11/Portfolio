Beat the Dealer

Edward O. Thorp

Overview

In Beat the Dealer, Thorp explains his system for beating the casinos. The strategies are always backed by stories of how they were tested and how the casinos and dealers responded. Thorp explains the standard rules to the reader, followed by the basic strategy that should be used when you do not have the edge over the casino. Following the basic strategy should bring you to a break-even point, meaning neither the casino nor you are really winning.

The goal is to upgrade the strategy once you have the advantage. Because blackjack is a game of dependent events — a card seen changes the probability of the next card seen — you can use advantage play. Thorp's early idea was to count fives: if all fives were gone, the player had the edge. From there the strategy developed into the point count system, where you add +1 for cards 2–6, +/- 0 for 7–9, and -1 for 10–A. A high count means you have the advantage, as the deck is rich in tens. From here you divide your count by the estimated number of cards remaining to get a ratio you can use to size your bets. Another system is the ten count, where you divide the number of non-tens by the number of tens left. The ratio starts at 2.25, and the lower it gets the greater your advantage.

The book then moves into how the casinos began changing rules and implementing measures to hinder advantage play. Back then, this included dealers cheating quite frequently, and the reader is made aware of what to look out for. The final chapter focuses on "science versus chance", where Thorp gives a quick history of how probability developed over the years, with mathematicians trying to decode apparent chance games, and how the largest market one can play is the exchange market.

Key Takeaways

While strategies have evolved, the main idea behind the point count system still prevails. Even if you have no interest in gambling, learning how math can beat games of chance gives you an advantage in thinking about probability and edge.

Who Should Read This

If you are a fan of Thorp, it is worth starting here to understand how Thorp got to his later successes.

Rating

4 out of 5. Some arguments could be backed more rigorously, but the book is short enough that it makes a great starting point.
